\title{\textbf{Why \texttt{gamma\_scan.py} recovers $\gamma$ biased low}\\[0.5em]
\large A diagnosis of the $c_0$ prior centroid and the
$c_0$--$\gamma$ degeneracy}
\author{Andr\'e Vitorelli}
\date{19 May 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The \texttt{scripts/gamma\_scan.py} pipeline systematically recovers
$\hat\gamma < \gamma_{\rm true}$, with a roughly constant offset across the
scanned range.  We trace the bias to a misalignment between the Gaussian prior
centred at $c_0 = 5.0$ and the actual Dutton \& Maccio (2014) prediction
$c_{\rm DM14}(M=10^{14}\,M_\odot, z=0.35) \approx 4.57$.  Through the
$c_0$--$\gamma$ degeneracy this $+0.039$~dex prior offset is converted into a
$\Delta\gamma \approx -0.14$ shift, given the empirical
$\langle\log_{10}(\dm/\dmref)\rangle \approx 0.287$ for the HOD-driven truth
catalogue.  We propose three orthogonal fixes (recentre $c_0$ on the DM14
truth; loosen the $c_0$ prior; or align $\dmref$ with the sample median) and
list the regression tests needed to confirm an unbiased recovery.
\end{abstract}

%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Observation}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The script
\texttt{scripts/gamma\_scan.py} repeats the analysis of the unbiased combined
recovery notebook (DES~Y3-like redshift distributions, triaxial NFW lensing
with Rayleigh+$\delta$ miscentering, radial cut at
$R\ge 0.5$\,Mpc, $n_{\rm live}=400$, $N=10\,000$ clusters per run) for a list
of $\gamma_{\rm true}$ values, and saves per-gamma JSON summaries plus a
$\gamma_{\rm true}$--vs--$\hat\gamma$ figure.  In every observed run, the
recovered posterior median $\hat\gamma$ falls below $\gamma_{\rm true}$ by
roughly a constant offset, of order $0.1$--$0.2$ across the scanned range.

The fit holds the following parameters
\emph{fixed} (not sampled) and at the simulation-truth values, so they cannot
be the source of any bias:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item miscentered fraction $\mathrm{FMISS}_i = 1-\fcen = 0.36$,
  \item miscentering scale $\sigmaoff = 0.32\,h^{-1}\,\mathrm{Mpc}$,
  \item the radial cut $R \ge 0.5\,\mathrm{Mpc}$,
  \item the DES~Y3 cluster and source $n(z)$.
\end{itemize}

The five sampled parameters are $(c_0, \alpha, \beta, \gamma, \log
M_{\rm norm})$ under explicit \texttt{scipy.stats} priors:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  c_0 &\sim \mathcal{N}( 5.0, 0.5), & \alpha &\sim \mathcal{N}(-0.1, 0.05),\\
  \beta &\sim \mathcal{N}(-0.4, 0.2), & \gamma &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1),\\
  \log M_{\rm norm} &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.1). &&
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:priors}
\end{equation}

%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{The dominant bias mechanism: misaligned $c_0$ prior}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Truth value of $c_0$ in the parametric model}

The truth concentrations are constructed (see \texttt{build\_truth\_catalog})
as the DM14 \citep{DuttonMaccio2014} prediction modulated by the magnitude-gap
shift:
\begin{equation}
  \log_{10} c_{\rm truth} = \log_{10} c_{\rm DM14}(z, M) + \gamma_{\rm true}\,\log_{10}\frac{\dm}{\dmref}.
\end{equation}
The parametric fit model is
\begin{equation}
  \log_{10} c = \log_{10} c_0 + \alpha\,(\log_{10}M - 14)
              + \beta\,\log_{10}\!\frac{1+z}{1+\zref}
              + \gamma\,\log_{10}\!\frac{\dm}{\dmref}.
  \label{eq:param}
\end{equation}
At the parametric model's reference point $(M=10^{14}\,M_\odot,\ z=\zref=0.35,\ \dm=\dmref)$,
the DM14 formula gives
\begin{equation}
  c_0^{\rm truth}
    = 10^{a(\zref) + 2\,b(\zref)} = 4.571,
\end{equation}
with $a(z) = 0.52 + 0.385\exp(-0.617\,z^{1.21})$ and
$b(z) = -0.101 + 0.026\,z$ (the DM14 coefficient functions).  Numerically:
\begin{equation}
  a(0.35) = 0.843,\quad b(0.35) = -0.092,\quad \log_{10} c_0^{\rm truth} = 0.660.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The $c_0$ prior is offset $\sim$one standard deviation above truth}

The prior $c_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(5.0, 0.5)$ has logarithmic centre and width
\begin{equation}
  \log_{10} 5.0 \approx 0.699,\quad
  \sigma_{\log c_0}^{\rm prior} \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl[\log_{10}5.5 - \log_{10}4.5\bigr] \approx 0.044\,\mathrm{dex}.
\end{equation}
The prior offset relative to the DM14 truth is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \dlog c_0^{\rm prior}
    \equiv \log_{10} 5.0 - \log_{10} c_0^{\rm truth}
    = +0.039\,\mathrm{dex},
  \label{eq:c0_offset}
\end{equation}
which is just under $1\,\sigma$ of the prior — informative enough to pull
the posterior, but not extreme.

\subsection{The $c_0$--$\gamma$ degeneracy converts a $c_0$ offset into a $\gamma$ shift}

The data constrains the four scaling parameters jointly along a near-banana
manifold defined by the equation that keeps each cluster's predicted
$\log_{10}c$ unchanged:
\begin{equation}
  \dlog c_0\;+\;\Delta\alpha\,(\log_{10}M_i - 14)\;
            +\;\Delta\beta\,\log_{10}\!\frac{1+z_i}{1+\zref}\;
            +\;\Delta\gamma\,\log_{10}\!\frac{\dm_i}{\dmref}\;\approx\;0
\end{equation}
for every cluster $i$.  Averaging over the sample, and noting that the
$(\log_{10}M-14)$ and $\log_{10}(1+z)/(1+\zref)$ averages are close to zero by
construction (the truth catalogue is broad over both), we have to first order
\begin{equation}
  \dlog c_0 + \Delta\gamma\,\bigl\langle\log_{10}(\dm/\dmref)\bigr\rangle \approx 0,
  \label{eq:degeneracy}
\end{equation}
so that any shift in $c_0$ along the degenerate direction is compensated by
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\Delta\gamma \approx -\,\dfrac{\dlog c_0}{\bigl\langle\log_{10}(\dm/\dmref)\bigr\rangle}\;.}
  \label{eq:bias_formula}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Empirical $\langle\log_{10}(\dm/\dmref)\rangle$ for the HOD sample}

Running \texttt{destacking.simulation.generate\_truth\_catalog} with the same
parameters used by \texttt{gamma\_scan.py} ($N=2000$ pool, $z \in [0.2,0.65]$,
$\log_{10}M \in [13.5, 15]$, default HOD), the magnitude-gap distribution
peaks at $\dm\approx 0.24$~mag (median) and has mean $\sim 0.36$~mag.  With
$\dmref = 0.1$\,mag the sample average of the gamma regressor is
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\langle\log_{10}(\dm/0.1)\bigr\rangle = 0.287.
  \label{eq:sample_avg}
\end{equation}
This is the crucial number: the magnitude-gap regressor has a fairly small
mean because the HOD-driven $\dm$ distribution sits not far above $\dmref$.

\subsection{Predicted $\gamma$ bias}

Combining Eqs.~(\ref{eq:c0_offset}), (\ref{eq:bias_formula}), and
(\ref{eq:sample_avg}):
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\gamma_{\rm pred} \approx -\frac{0.039}{0.287} \approx -0.136.
  \label{eq:gamma_pred}
\end{equation}
\textbf{This is the dominant bias: the misaligned $c_0$ prior, working through
the $c_0$--$\gamma$ degeneracy, biases $\hat\gamma$ low by roughly $0.14$.}
The bias is approximately additive: $\hat\gamma \approx \gamma_{\rm true}
- 0.14$, independent of $\gamma_{\rm true}$ to leading order.  This matches
the observed roughly-constant low offset in the scan plots.

%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Sub-dominant contributors}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We checked three additional prior or data effects.  All are small.

\paragraph{$\alpha$ prior offset.}
The DM14 mass slope at $z=\zref$ is $b(\zref) = -0.092$, and the prior is
$\alpha \sim \mathcal{N}(-0.1, 0.05)$.  The prior centroid is therefore at
$-0.008$ from truth, well within $1\sigma$.  Through the $\alpha$--$\gamma$
covariance (which is small because $\log_{10}M$ and $\log_{10}(\dm/\dmref)$ are
weakly correlated through the HOD), this contributes a $\gamma$ shift
$\lesssim 10^{-2}$.

\paragraph{$\beta$ prior offset.}
A least-squares fit of $\log_{10}c_{\rm DM14}(M=10^{14}, z)$ vs
$\log_{10}((1+z)/(1+\zref))$ over $z\in[0.21, 0.64]$ gives an effective
$\beta_{\rm DM14}^{\rm eff} \approx -0.455$, while the prior is
$\beta \sim \mathcal{N}(-0.4, 0.2)$.  The offset is $+0.055$ but the prior
sigma is $0.2$, so the prior is loose and the posterior is essentially
data-driven; the propagated $\gamma$ shift is again $\lesssim 10^{-2}$.

\paragraph{Gamma prior contraction.}
With $N=10\,000$ destacked clusters and the radial cut, the data sigma on
$\gamma$ is of order $\sigma_\gamma^{\rm data} \approx 0.03$--$0.05$.  The
prior $\gamma \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ contracts the posterior toward zero by a
factor $1/(1 + \sigma_\gamma^{\rm data\,2}/\sigma_\gamma^{\rm prior\,2})
\approx 1 - 0.0025$; a $\sim 0.25\%$ contraction is negligible compared to
the prior-offset bias above.

\paragraph{Residual triaxiality at $R > \Rmin$.}
The radial cut $R \ge 0.5$\,Mpc reduces the spherical-vs-triaxial mismatch but
does not eliminate it.  Any residual would tend to push $c_0$ \emph{low} and
$\gamma$ \emph{high} (the original direction without the cut), which acts
\emph{against} the prior-offset bias above and so cannot account for the
observed low $\gamma$.

%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Proposed fixes}
\label{sec:fixes}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Any of the following will remove the dominant bias.  They are not mutually
exclusive — Fix~A is the simplest and is recommended first.

\subsection*{Fix A — recentre the $c_0$ prior on the DM14 truth}

Change the prior in \texttt{scripts/gamma\_scan.py} (and in the corresponding
notebook cell):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
& Before & After \\
\midrule
\texttt{c0} & \texttt{norm(loc=5.0, scale=0.5)} & \texttt{norm(loc=4.6, scale=0.5)} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
This single change removes the $+0.039$~dex offset and zeroes the leading-order
$\gamma$ bias.  $c_0$ posteriors will move down by $\sim 0.4$ in concentration
units, leaving the recovery of $\gamma$ centred on the truth.

A slightly more principled variant is to recentre on the OLS-extracted truth
$c_0^{\rm OLS}$ reported by \texttt{build\_truth\_catalog}, which already
accounts for the sample-specific $z$ distribution.  For the DES~Y3-like
$P(z) \propto z^2$ on $[0.2, 0.65]$, $c_0^{\rm OLS}$ comes out very close to
$4.6$.

\subsection*{Fix B — loosen the $c_0$ prior}

A simple alternative is to widen the prior so that the data, not the prior,
determines $c_0$:
\begin{center}
\texttt{c0} ~ \texttt{norm(loc=5.0, scale=2.0)} \quad or \quad
\texttt{c0} ~ \texttt{uniform(loc=1.0, scale=10.0)}.
\end{center}
With $\sigma^{\rm prior}_{\log c_0}$ comparable to or larger than the data
constraint on $c_0$, the posterior median migrates onto the data preferred
value and the propagated $\gamma$ shift vanishes.  This is the appropriate
fix if one does not want to assume DM14 a priori.

\subsection*{Fix C — align $\dmref$ with the sample median}

Equation~(\ref{eq:bias_formula}) says the bias is proportional to
$\langle\log_{10}(\dm/\dmref)\rangle$.  Setting
\begin{equation}
  \dmref = \mathrm{median}(\dm) \approx 0.24\,\mathrm{mag}
\end{equation}
makes the regressor mean equal to zero, decouples $\gamma$ from $c_0$ at
leading order, and removes the bias even if the $c_0$ prior remains
misaligned.  This change is also analytically cleaner: $c_0$ then literally
is the concentration normalisation at the typical sample magnitude gap,
rather than at an extreme $\dm$ value where almost no clusters live.

%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Regression tests}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

To confirm the diagnosis the following short scans should be run.  Each one
is the existing \texttt{gamma\_scan.py} pipeline with a single configuration
change.

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Baseline check.}  Rerun the scan with the current
        configuration and tabulate $\hat\gamma - \gamma_{\rm true}$ for each
        scanned $\gamma_{\rm true}$.  Confirm a roughly constant offset
        $\approx -0.14$.
  \item \textbf{Fix~A: recentre $c_0$.} Change \texttt{c0.loc=5.0} $\to$
        \texttt{c0.loc=4.6} (or to $c_0^{\rm OLS}$).  Expect the offset to
        drop to $|\hat\gamma - \gamma_{\rm true}| \lesssim 0.01$.
  \item \textbf{Fix~B: loosen $c_0$.} Change \texttt{c0.scale=0.5} $\to$
        \texttt{c0.scale=2.0}.  Expect the offset to drop similarly, at the
        cost of slightly wider $c_0$ posteriors.
  \item \textbf{Fix~C: shift $\dmref$.} Set \texttt{DELTA\_M\_REF =
        median(truth['delta\_m'])} (sample-dependent).  Expect the offset to
        drop similarly; truth values should now read off as
        $c_0^{\rm truth}$ at the new reference $\dm$.
  \item \textbf{Posterior credibility check.}  After Fix~A, verify that the
        $\gamma$ posterior covers $\gamma_{\rm true}$ at the expected 68\%
        rate over the scan.
\end{enumerate}

%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusions}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The observed low bias in $\hat\gamma$ from \texttt{gamma\_scan.py} is not a
data-side systematic — neither triaxiality, miscentering, the redshift
distributions, nor the gamma prior breadth can produce it.  It originates in
the $c_0$ prior, whose Gaussian centre of $5.0$ sits $+0.039$~dex above the
actual DM14 prediction $c_0^{\rm truth} \approx 4.57$ at the parametric
reference point.  Acting through the $c_0$--$\gamma$ degeneracy and the
sample-mean of the magnitude-gap regressor
$\langle\log_{10}(\dm/\dmref)\rangle \approx 0.287$, this offset is converted
into an additive shift
\[
  \Delta\gamma \approx -\,\frac{+0.039}{0.287} \approx -0.14,
\]
quantitatively consistent with the scan plots.  The fix is a one-line prior
recentring (or a sample-dependent choice of $\dmref$); after applying it the
recovered $\hat\gamma$ should track $\gamma_{\rm true}$ with biases at the
$10^{-2}$ level set only by the sub-dominant $\alpha,\beta$ prior offsets and
the residual sub-bin triaxiality effects.
