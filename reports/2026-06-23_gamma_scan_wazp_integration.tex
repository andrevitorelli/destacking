simulation pipeline, notebook integration, and the role of the

\author{destacking project}
\date{2026 June 23}

\begin{abstract}
This report documents three pieces of work that together make the
destacking $\gamma$-recovery scan run on a fully WaZP-matched
simulation, feed the same simulation back into the
\verb|wazp_lensing_signals.ipynb| and \verb|wazp_mass_richness_by_delta_m.ipynb|
analysis notebooks, and resolve the question of whether the global
$\DS$-amplitude correction $A$ should be retained. The simulation
pipeline produces 15,000 truth clusters drawn from a Tinker-08 mass
function with a Zheng-07 HOD and a per-cluster magnitude gap derived
from a power-law satellite LF, with observable richness $\Lambda$
obtained by inverting the McClintock+18 mass-richness relation. The
WaZP selection ($\Lambda > 25$, $\Delta m > 0$, $0.10 \le z < 0.60$) is
applied at the truth level, and per-cluster shear catalogues are
injected with the spherical NFW model at the WaZP-matched source
density $\rho = 7.5\,\mathrm{gal}/\mathrm{arcmin}^{2}$. Two 51-point
$\gamma$-scans were run --- one with $A$ free under a tight
$\mathcal{N}(0.80, 0.02)$ prior, one with $A$ pinned at 1.0 ---
producing RMS biases of 0.310 and 0.334 respectively, indistinguishable
at the per-fit $\langle\sigma_{\rm boot}\rangle \approx 0.033$
precision. We therefore drop the amplitude correction from all future
$\DS$ fits.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}
\label{sec:scope}

Three deliverables, building on one another:

\begin{enumerate}
\item A WaZP-matched simulation pipeline in
      \verb|scripts/gamma_scan.py| that builds the truth catalogue, the
      observable richness, the magnitude gap, and the per-cluster
      lensing background -- all from physically motivated distributions
      -- and writes outputs in two complementary forms: \texttt{npz}
      checkpoints inside the $\gamma$-scan tree, and notebook-compatible
      \texttt{.fits} + \texttt{.pkl} files that the analysis notebooks
      can load as if they were the real WaZP catalogue.
\item A \verb|SIMS=True| toggle in both
      \verb|wazp_lensing_signals.ipynb| and
      \verb|wazp_mass_richness_by_delta_m.ipynb|, so the same
      cell-by-cell analysis can run on the simulated catalogue without
      any code changes.
\item Two 51-point $\gamma$-scans demonstrating that the global
      $\DS$-amplitude correction $A$ has negligible effect on
      $\gamma$-recovery, motivating dropping it from the fit.
\end{enumerate}

The cluster catalogue follows the same upstream selection as
\verb|wazp_mass_richness_by_delta_m.ipynb|: $\Lambda > 25$, $\Delta m > 0$
(positive magnitude gap), and $0.10 \le z_{\rm cluster} < 0.60$. Truth
masses are drawn from a Tinker-08 mass function restricted to
$\log_{10}(M/\Msun) \in [14.0, 15.0]$ to keep the oversampling
efficiency $\sim$30\% at the $\Lambda > 25$ cut.


\section{Simulation pipeline}
\label{sec:sim}

The pipeline lives in \verb|scripts/gamma_scan.py| (with helper
functions in \verb|destacking.simulation|) and runs once per
$\gamma$ value. Its steps are:

\subsection{Truth catalogue}

For each $\gamma$ index $g$, we oversample
\verb|destacking.simulation.generate_truth_catalog| in batches until
$N_{\rm cl} = 15\,000$ clusters survive the WaZP selection. For each
draw of $N$ candidates:

\paragraph{Mass.}
$\log_{10}(M_{\rm halo})$ is sampled from a Tinker-08
mass function restricted to $[14.0, 15.0]$,
\begin{equation}
\log_{10} M \;\sim\; n_{\rm Tinker08}(M, z),
\qquad z \sim \mathcal{U}(0.10, 0.60).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Concentration.}
The NFW concentration follows Dutton \& Maccio (2014) with a 0.1\,dex
lognormal scatter:
\begin{equation}
\log_{10} c_{\rm DM}(M, z)
\;=\; a(z) + b(z)\,\log_{10}\!\bigl(M / 10^{12}\Msun\bigr),
\quad
c \;=\; c_{\rm DM} \cdot 10^{\mathcal{N}(0,\,0.1)}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Magnitude gap.}
A Zheng-07 HOD assigns satellite counts; satellite luminosities are
drawn from a power-law luminosity function $\phi(L) \propto L^{\alpha}$
with $\alpha = -1.2$, truncated below $L_{\rm min} = 10^{8}\Lsun$.
The brightest satellite luminosity sets the magnitude gap,
\begin{equation}
\Delta m \;=\; 2.5\,\log_{10}\!\bigl(L_{\rm BCG} / L_{\rm sat,\,max}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $L_{\rm BCG}$ is the BCG luminosity from the Zheng et al.\ 2007
prescription applied to the truth mass.

\paragraph{Observable richness $\Lambda$.}
Inverting the McClintock+18 mass-richness relation,
\begin{equation}
\log_{10}\Lambda
\;=\;
\log_{10}\lpiv \;+\;
\frac{\log_{10} M - \log_{10} M_{0} - g_{z}\log_{10}\bigl(\tfrac{1+z}{1+\zpiv}\bigr)}{F_{\Lambda}},
\end{equation}
with $\lpiv = 40$, $\zpiv = 0.35$, $\log_{10} M_{0} = 14.489$,
$F_{\Lambda} = 1.356$, $g_{z} = -0.30$, and a Poisson noise wrap
$\Lambda_{\rm obs} \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\Lambda_{\rm pred})$.

\paragraph{Selection.}
We keep
$\Lambda > 25$, $\Delta m > 0$, $z \in [0.10, 0.60)$, and the
overall efficiency $\sim 35\%$ leaves $N_{\rm cl} = 15\,000$ surviving
clusters after $\sim$3 batches of 43\,000 draws.

\paragraph{$\Delta m$--$c$ modulation.}
The single dial driving the $\gamma$-scan is a post-hoc rescaling of
the truth concentration as a function of $\Delta m$,
\begin{equation}
c \;\to\; c \cdot \biggl(\frac{\Delta m}{\dmref}\biggr)^{\gtrue},
\qquad
\dmref = 0.1\,\mathrm{mag},
\label{eq:dmc}
\end{equation}
applied after the truth catalogue is otherwise complete. $\gtrue = 0$
recovers the unmodified Dutton-Maccio concentration.

\subsection{Per-cluster shear catalogues}

For each surviving cluster $i$ we generate an independent metacal-style
source catalogue with
\verb|xlensing.sims.make_simple_random_cat|, at the WaZP-matched density
\begin{equation}
\rho_{\rm gal} = 7.5\,\arcmin^{-2},
\quad
\sigma_e = 0.27,
\quad
z_{\rm src} \in (0, 2.5),
\end{equation}
uniformly distributed inside a square of half-width
$\text{WIDTH\_RAD} = (R_{\rm cone} + 0.3\Mpc) / D_{A}(z_{i})$, which
contains the 5\,Mpc cone with a small margin. We inject the spherical
NFW shear at the cluster's $(z_{i}, M_{i}, c_{i})$ with
\verb|xlensing.sims.apply_NFW_shear_region|, then reduce with
\verb|metacal_cluster_lensing| at a 5\,Mpc cone radius.

The per-cluster reduction returns the 6-row background array
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{bg}_{i} \;=\;
\bigl[\,
\sigcrit,\;
e_{t},\;
e_{x},\;
W,\;
R\,[\Mpc],\;
M_{\rm bias}
\,\bigr],
\end{equation}
which is the input form expected by \verb|stacked_signal| and
\verb|single_cluster|. The radial grid is
\begin{equation}
R_{b} \;=\; \mathtt{np.logspace(-0.8,\,0.6,\,8)}
\;=\; [0.158,\, 0.251,\, \ldots,\, 3.981]\Mpc.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Notebook-compatible outputs}

For each $\gamma$ index, a helper
\verb|_write_notebook_compat_sim| writes three files into
\verb|notebooks/data/|:
\begin{align}
& \texttt{sim\_g\{idx:03d\}\_real\_data.fits}
&& \text{cluster catalogue (ID, RA, DEC, ZPHOT, NGALS, DELTA\_M\_I, DELTA\_M\_I\_ERR)}\\
& \texttt{sim\_g\{idx:03d\}\_bgs.pkl}
&& \text{dict: ID $\to$ per-cluster bg array (6 $\times$ N$_{\rm gal}$, float32)}\\
& \texttt{sim\_g\{idx:03d\}\_metadata.json}
&& \text{$\gamma_{\rm true}$, seed, density, generation timestamp}
\end{align}
The catalogue schema matches \verb|wazp_real_data.fits| column-for-column;
positions are placed on a non-overlapping RA/Dec grid at 0.6$^{\circ}$
spacing so that any geometric lookup the notebooks perform stays
well-defined, even though the actual bgs are pre-computed and looked up
by ID.

The simulated lensing run is the bottleneck: at 15 workers the per-cluster
shear injection + reduction takes $\approx 13$\,min for 15\,000 clusters.


\section{Notebook integration: the SIMS toggle}
\label{sec:notebooks}

Both analysis notebooks now expose two switches at the top of their
\verb|imports| cell:
\begin{verbatim}
SIMS       = False    # True to run on a simulated catalogue
SIM_PREFIX = 'sim'    # picks up sim_real_data.fits + sim_bgs.pkl
\end{verbatim}
When \verb|SIMS=True|, every downstream path that consumes the WaZP
catalogue or the metacal source pre-filter is re-routed to the
sim equivalents:
\begin{itemize}
\item \verb|WAZP_REAL| $\to$ \verb|data/{SIM_PREFIX}_real_data.fits|
\item \verb|SIM_BGS_PKL| points to \verb|data/{SIM_PREFIX}_bgs.pkl|
\item All output files (stacks, fits, M--$\lambda$, c--M pickles) get the
      \verb|sim_| prefix.
\end{itemize}
The metacal pre-filter (\verb|§2|), BallTree build (\verb|§3|), and
spatial cone queries (\verb|§5| worker) all short-circuit when SIMS is
on: the worker dispatches to a dict lookup
\verb|SIM_BGS_BY_ID[cluster_id]| instead of querying a BallTree.

For \verb|wazp_lensing_signals.ipynb|, the cluster-scatter
diagnostic (\verb|cluster-scatter| cell) was patched separately to
skip the source-galaxy redshift histogram in SIMS mode (the source
arrays are \verb|None| there). The y-axis of the \verb|compare| cell
(§9, avg-of-individuals vs stack residual) was tightened to
$[-150, 100]\%$ to match the dynamic range needed when the sim shows
narrow residuals but the real data plunges past $-100\%$.

The user picks a specific $\gamma$ by setting \verb|SIM_PREFIX = 'sim_g025'|
for $\gtrue = 0$, \verb|'sim_g050'| for $\gtrue = +1$, etc. (51 sims
are on disk; see Section~\ref{sec:scan}).


\section{The $\gamma$-scan: configuration and the result-summary plot}
\label{sec:scan}

\verb|scripts/gamma_scan.py| was updated to run the scan on the new
WaZP-style catalogue and to produce a richer summary plot:

\paragraph{Configuration.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \verb|N_GAMMA = 51|, $\gtrue$ linearly spaced over $[-1, +1]$.
\item \verb|N_CLUSTERS = 15000|, \verb|DENSITY = 7.5|,
      \verb|RADII = np.logspace(-0.8, 0.6, 8)|.
\item \verb|BOOTSTRAP = 0|, \verb|N_BOOTSTRAP = 1|: one full-sample
      nautilus fit per $\gamma$. The user decided that the scatter
      \emph{across} $\gtrue$ already quantifies the recovery bias, so
      post-hoc bootstrap draws are unnecessary.
\item Reported error per fit: 16/84 half-width of the single
      posterior on $\gamma$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Summary plot.}
\verb|plot_gamma_scan_results(scan_results, run_dir, bin_size=10)|
produces a two-panel figure (top: $\gest$ vs $\gtrue$; bottom: residual)
overlaying three layers in each panel:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Light-blue individual fits} with $\sigma_{\rm boot}$
      errorbars (faded so the trend is visible through them).
\item \textbf{Purple binned-median line} with circles. The data are
      sorted by $\gtrue$ and grouped into bins of $b = 10$ consecutive
      points; the median and the 16th/84th percentiles inside each bin
      give the central curve and the shaded 68\% credible band.
\item \textbf{Crimson linear-regression line}
      $\gest = (1 + s)\,\gtrue + \delta$ fit by OLS to the unbinned
      data, with $s$ and $\delta$ reported in the legend.
\end{itemize}
Because the user later asked for the OLS-truth crosses to be removed
(the black dashed perfect-recovery line is sufficient), they were
dropped from the plot. The function lives in
\verb|gamma_scan.py| and is rerun automatically at the end of every
scan, so future scans get the same layout.


\section{Per-gamma posterior corner plots}
\label{sec:corner}

\verb|run_one_bootstrap| was extended to save a corner plot per fit.
The \verb|gamma_posterior.npz| inside each bootstrap subdirectory now
carries the full posterior (all PARAM\_ORDER columns) under the
\verb|all_samples| key, alongside the weights and the marginal $\gamma$
samples. After each fit, \verb|corner.corner| renders a 5-parameter
corner on the live posterior, with:
\begin{itemize}
\item titles showing 16/50/84 quantiles,
\item filled 1$\sigma$/2$\sigma$ contours,
\item smoothed marginals,
\item crimson truth lines for the four parameters with an OLS-derived
      truth ($c_{0}$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$).
\end{itemize}
Each corner lands at
\verb|simulation_g{idx:02d}_gamma{±X.X}/bootstrap_000/posterior_corner.png|.
The corner is generated regardless of whether bootstrapping is enabled.

Figure~\ref{fig:corner-g0} shows the corner for $\gtrue = 0$ from
the second scan. The diagnostic surfaces non-trivial posterior
structure: $c_{0}$ is visibly bimodal, $\beta$ jams against the upper
edge of its $\mathcal{N}(-0.4, 0.2)$ prior, and the $\gamma$ posterior
is essentially Gaussian but offset to $\gest = -0.291 \pm 0.053$. The
posterior is not a posterior-shape pathology; it is a real failure mode
of the joint fit that the population scan made visible.

\section{Two $\gamma$-scans: $A$ free vs.\ $A$ fixed}
\label{sec:Atest}

\subsection{Set-up}

Two scans were run back-to-back on exactly the same simulation
pipeline, differing only in whether the global $\DS$ amplitude
correction $A$ was sampled or fixed at 1.0. The fitting model that the
$\gamma$-scan uses is, schematically,
\begin{equation}
\widehat\DS_{\rm model}(R; \theta)
\;=\;
A \cdot \DS_{\rm NFW}\!\left(R \mid M(\Lambda, z; \log M_{\rm norm}),
       c(M, z, \Delta m; c_{0}, \alpha, \beta, \gamma)\right),
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
with the magnitude-gap modulation
02............................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................................
Run~A had a prior
$A \sim \mathcal{N}(0.80, 0.02)$, centred on the simulation-measured
spherical-vs-triaxial $\sim$20\% deficit. Run~B omitted $A$ from the
prior dict so that \verb|destacking.fitting.run_sampler_combined|
internally fixed $A = 1.0$. Both used the same priors on
$\{c_{0}, \alpha, \beta, \gamma, \log M_{\rm norm}\}$.

\subsection{Result}

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{l c c c c}
\toprule
run & $A$ status & RMS bias of $\gest$ & $\langle\sigma_{\rm boot}\rangle$ & total wall \\
\midrule
\verb|run_2026-06-12T12-49-51| & $A \sim \mathcal{N}(0.80, 0.02)$ & 0.310 & 0.034 & 7h 36m \\
\verb|run_2026-06-13T10-26-14| & $A = 1.0$ (fixed)                & 0.334 & 0.033 & 7h 34m \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Summary of the two $\gamma$-scans. The difference in RMS bias
is 0.024 --- smaller than the typical per-fit
$\sigma_{\rm boot} \approx 0.033$ --- and the mean per-fit
uncertainty is identical to 1\%. Fixing $A$ at unity has negligible
effect on $\gamma$-recovery.}
\label{tab:Acompare}
\end{table}

The reproducible failure modes are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Negative tail ($\gtrue \lesssim -0.5$).} A floor at
      $\gest \approx -0.50$ that the fit refuses to break through.
      Visible as a flat plateau in both panels of
      Fig.~\ref{fig:Acompare}. Not affected by $A$.
\item \textbf{Positive tail ($\gtrue \gtrsim +0.5$).} Multi-modal
      likelihood; nautilus occasionally lands at $\gest \approx +1.4$
      with $\sigma_{\rm boot} \approx 10^{-3}$ (a "confident" wrong
      mode). The five $\gtrue$ values affected (+0.32, +0.52, +0.64,
      +0.72, +0.88) are exactly the same in both scans. Again, not
      sensitive to $A$.
\item \textbf{Mid-range ($|\gtrue| \lesssim 0.5$).} The recovery
      tightens; the binned-median trace runs within $\pm 0.05$ of
      truth.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Decision}

Since the amplitude correction has no measurable effect on
$\gamma$-recovery and was already at the edge of its prior in many
fits, we drop it. Operationally:
\begin{itemize}
\item In \verb|scripts/gamma_scan.py|, the \verb|PRIORS| dict no
      longer contains \verb|'A'|, and \verb|PARAM_ORDER| omits it.
      \verb|destacking.fitting.run_sampler_combined| fixes
      $A = 1.0$ internally when the key is absent.
\item This is recorded as the auto-memory entry
      \verb|feedback_no_amplitude_correction.md| so future fit scripts
      and notebooks inherit the directive.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary}

\begin{itemize}
\item \verb|scripts/gen_sim_data_for_notebooks.py| and the
      \verb|_write_notebook_compat_sim| hook inside
      \verb|gamma_scan.py| produce 15\,000-cluster WaZP-style sims with
      per-cluster bgs, ready to be consumed by both analysis notebooks
      via the \verb|SIMS=True| toggle.
\item The \verb|gamma_scan.py| pipeline was rewritten to oversample
      truth until the WaZP selection passes 15\,000 clusters, run a
      single nautilus fit per $\gamma$, and emit a per-gamma posterior
      corner plot. The summary recovery plot now overlays a binned
      median, 68\%~CL band, and linear regression.
\item Two 51-point scans confirm that fixing $A = 1.0$ vs.\ leaving it
      free under a tight $\mathcal{N}(0.80, 0.02)$ prior changes the
      RMS $\gamma$-recovery bias by 0.024 --- below the per-fit noise
      floor. We drop $A$.
\item 51 sets of \verb|sim_g{000..050}_real_data.fits| +
      \verb|sim_g{000..050}_bgs.pkl| are on disk, ready for any
      per-$\gamma$ inspection inside the notebooks.
\end{itemize}
