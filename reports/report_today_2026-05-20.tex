\end{enumerate}

\section{Robustness fixes to the inference pipeline}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Sampler timeout and verbose progress}

When the $\gamma$ prior is widened, nautilus occasionally falls into a
slow-convergence regime: \texttt{f\_live} stalls and the sampler refuses to
finish.  Two changes:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \texttt{run\_sampler\_combined} gained a new \texttt{timeout}
        kwarg (seconds), passed straight through to nautilus'
        \texttt{Sampler.run()}.  A stuck sampler now returns rather than
        hangs indefinitely.
  \item \texttt{verbose=True} is now the default in both
        \texttt{scripts/gamma\_scan.py} and \texttt{scripts/gamma\_repeat.py}
        so that nautilus prints its live-point statistics; a glance at
        \texttt{f\_live} reveals the slow-convergence regime immediately.
  \item Per-realisation checkpoints (\texttt{nautilus\_checkpoint.hdf5})
        let a crashed sampler resume from disk on the next invocation
        instead of restarting from scratch.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Failure handling: don't count, delete, retry}

If a single realisation fails for any reason (sampler crash, numerical
glitch, user interrupt), \texttt{scripts/gamma\_repeat.py} now:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Removes the partial \texttt{realisation\_<idx>/} subdirectory with
        \texttt{shutil.rmtree(\ldots,~ignore\_errors=True)} so that a
        subsequent run does not mistake the half-written state for a cache
        hit.
  \item Does \emph{not} count the failed attempt toward the target
        \texttt{N\_REALISATIONS}; the while-loop simply keeps trying.
  \item Still includes the wall time of the failed attempt in the running
        ETA, and the empirical failure rate is folded in:
        \[
          \text{expected remaining attempts} =
          \frac{\text{remaining successes}}{\max(1-f_{\rm fail},\,10^{-3})}.
        \]
\end{itemize}

A safety cap of $4\times N$ total attempts prevents an infinite loop in
the worst case, and \texttt{KeyboardInterrupt} re-raises so $\mathrm{Ctrl{+}C}$
exits cleanly.

\subsection{NaN guards in the likelihood}

A wide $\gamma$ prior lets the sampler explore parameter values where
$10^{\log_{10}c}$ overflows to $+\infty$ in the forward model.  The
xlensing NFW propagates that as NaN.  \texttt{numpy.linalg.solve} silently
propagates NaN without raising; \texttt{cholog\_gaussian} was returning
NaN to nautilus, which then degenerated to \texttt{f\_live = NaN}.  Three
edits make the likelihood NaN-safe:

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \texttt{cholog\_gaussian} now skips any cluster whose residual
        contains NaN/$\pm\infty$, broadens the exception handler to
        \texttt{(LinAlgError, ValueError, FloatingPointError)}, validates
        the per-cluster and total log-likelihood, and \emph{never} returns
        NaN.
  \item \texttt{log\_likelihood}, \texttt{log\_likelihood\_mc},
        \texttt{log\_likelihood\_combined} all check
        \texttt{np.all(np.isfinite(model))} after the forward pass and
        \texttt{np.isfinite(ll)} after \texttt{cholog\_gaussian}, returning
        $-\infty$ on either failure.
  \item Verified end-to-end: an extreme draw
        $(\gamma{=}500,\,\dm_{\rm ref}{=}10^{-4})$ that produced an
        all-NaN forward model used to leak NaN into the chain; the patched
        likelihood returns $-\infty$ and nautilus treats the region as
        zero-probability.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Headline result.}  The mean recovery is unbiased to within
$0.02$, but every per-realisation posterior is a factor $\sim\!2.8\times$

problem is therefore fully on the fitting side, not in the simulation
truth.

\subsection{Diagnosis: what makes $\sigma_\gamma^{\rm post}$ too small}

Three structural fitting-side assumptions conspire.

\paragraph{1. Per-cluster covariance is shape-noise-only.}  Each
$\mathbf{C}_i$ is the diagonal Gaussian shape-noise matrix
\[
  \mathbf{C}_{i,bb} = \bigl(\sigma_e\,\bar\Sigma_{\rm cr,b}/\sqrt{n_{{\rm gal},b}}\bigr)^2.
\]
What is missing is the \emph{coherent} per-cluster amplitude offset
introduced by (i) triaxiality with isotropic LOS ($\sim\!20\text{--}30\%$
of $\dsig$ at intermediate $R$, correlated across all bins of the same
cluster) and (ii) the per-cluster realisation of miscentering vs.\ the
forward model's fixed-$\fcen$ correction ($\sim\!30\text{--}40\%$ peak
mismatch).  Because $\partial m_i/\partial\gamma \propto m_i$
approximately, the Fisher information on $\gamma$ that comes from
\emph{amplitude} is exactly the contribution that should be suppressed by
those coherent terms; the bare covariance double-counts it.

\paragraph{2. Tight DM14 priors break the $c_0$--$\gamma$ degeneracy.}
After Fix~A, $c_0\sim\mathcal{N}(4.57,0.05)$ pins $\log_{10}c_0$ to
$\sim\!0.005$ dex.  Marginalising over a degeneracy widens the marginal
posterior on $\gamma$; with this prior the degeneracy is essentially
broken at the prior level, so the $\gamma$ marginal stays narrow.

\paragraph{3. Independent-cluster likelihood.}
$\ln\mathcal{L}=\sum_i\ln\mathcal{L}_i$ assumes the per-cluster residuals
are uncorrelated.  Any shared systematic (the common source $n(z)$, a
global mass-richness shift, a common-mode triaxiality bias) is invisible
to this model.  Effective $N$ is smaller than the nominal $5000$, but the
Fisher formula uses the nominal value.

The three effects are not independent: of these, (1) is the largest
contributor.  Order-of-magnitude estimates of the integrated per-cluster
shape-noise amplitude ($\sim\!40\,M_\odot/\mathrm{pc}^2$) and the
triaxiality-plus-miscentering coherent amplitude
($\sim\!25\text{--}40\,M_\odot/\mathrm{pc}^2$) are comparable; including
the latter in the per-cluster cov could realistically widen
$\sigma_\gamma^{\rm post}$ by a factor of $\gtrsim\!2$.

\subsection{Fix \#2 implemented: rank-1 covariance inflation}
\label{sec:rank1}

\paragraph{Physical motivation.}
Cluster-to-cluster, the simulated $\dsig$ signal contains
two contributions that are NOT shape noise and are NOT in the per-cluster
covariance:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \emph{Halo triaxiality with isotropic line of sight.}  Pioneering
        $N$-body and lensing-bias studies show that fitting a spherical
        NFW to a triaxial halo yields a per-cluster amplitude scatter
        roughly proportional to the unscattered $\dsig$, of order
        $20\!-\!30\%$ at intermediate radii
        \citep{JingSuto2002,Allgood2006,Corless2007,Becker2011,Oguri2012}.
        Crucially this scatter is \emph{correlated across radial bins of
        the same cluster}: a halo seen with its major axis closer to the
        line of sight has \emph{all} of its bins enhanced; a perpendicular
        line of sight depresses them.
  \item \emph{Per-cluster realisation of miscentering.}  The forward
        model uses the population-averaged Rayleigh+$\delta$ correction
        with the same $(\fcen,\sigmaoff)$ for every cluster.  An
        \emph{individual} cluster either is centred or has a specific
        offset $R_{\rm mis}$; in either case the discrepancy with the
        population-average profile is \emph{also} coherent across radial
        bins of that one cluster (the entire inner peak is either
        present or smeared)
        \citep{Johnston2007,Rykoff2016,McClintock2018,Zhang2019}.
\end{itemize}
In Bayesian language the per-cluster data model should be
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{d}_i \;=\; \mathbf{m}_i(\boldsymbol\theta) \;+\; \xi_i\,\mathbf{m}_i(\boldsymbol\theta)
                    \;+\; \boldsymbol\epsilon_i,
  \label{eq:gen_model}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol\epsilon_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{C}_i)$
is the bin-diagonal shape-noise Gaussian and $\xi_i \sim
\mathcal{N}(0, \mathrm{coh}^2)$ is a per-cluster scalar nuisance that
captures the coherent amplitude shift induced by the two effects above.
Marginalising the multivariate Gaussian over $\xi_i$ in closed form
\citep[\S 1.6]{Bishop2006} produces an effective per-cluster covariance
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{C}_{{\rm eff},i} \;=\; \mathbf{C}_i \;+\;
            \mathrm{coh}^2 \,\mathbf{m}_i\,\mathbf{m}_i^\top,
  \label{eq:rank1}
\end{equation}
which is the rank-1 inflation we introduced today.  Writing
$\mathbf{u}_i \equiv \mathrm{coh}\cdot\mathbf{m}_i$, this is exactly the
form analytically equivalent to fitting a free per-cluster amplitude
nuisance with a Gaussian prior of width \texttt{coh\_amplitude} on its
fractional deviation from unity — the technique used in cluster cosmology
to absorb intrinsic-scatter contributions to $\Sigma_{\rm crit}$ and
mass-richness modelling \citep[e.g.][]{Mantz2010,Mantz2015}.  The same
"add a model-proportional rank-1 term to the covariance" idea is the
standard remedy for un-modelled multiplicative systematics in cosmic-shear
two-point analyses \citep[][\S 4.5]{Heymans2013}, with the role of
$\mathbf{m}_i$ played there by the data vector itself.

\paragraph{Sherman--Morrison Cholesky update.}
A dense inversion of Eq.\,(\ref{eq:rank1}) for $N=5000$ clusters at
every nautilus likelihood call would be prohibitive.  The Sherman--Morrison
identity \citep{ShermanMorrison1950}
\begin{equation}
  (\mathbf{A} + \mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}^\top)^{-1} \;=\;
   \mathbf{A}^{-1}
   \;-\; \frac{\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{u}\,\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{A}^{-1}}
              {1 + \mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{u}},
  \label{eq:sm}
\end{equation}
combined with the matrix determinant lemma
$|\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{u}\mathbf{u}^\top| = |\mathbf{A}|\,(1+\mathbf{u}^\top\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{u})$
\citep[][Eq.\,1.5.13]{Harville2008}, allows us to reuse the existing
Cholesky factor $\mathbf{L}_i$ of $\mathbf{C}_i$ without ever building or
inverting the dense matrix.  Concretely, with
$\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathbf{r}$ and $\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathbf{u}$
obtained from two cheap triangular solves,
\begin{align}
  \ln|\mathbf{C}_{\rm eff}|     &= 2\sum_j\!\ln L_{jj} + \ln\!\bigl(1 + \mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{w}\bigr), \label{eq:logdet}\\
  \mathbf{r}^\top\mathbf{C}_{\rm eff}^{-1}\mathbf{r} &= \mathbf{y}^\top\mathbf{y}
       - \frac{(\mathbf{y}^\top\mathbf{w})^2}{1 + \mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{w}}.
  \label{eq:quad_sm}
\end{align}
The per-cluster cost goes from $\mathcal{O}(k^3)$ (the original Cholesky
factorisation of $\mathbf{C}_i$, $k=N_{\rm bin}$) to that same value plus
\emph{one} additional $\mathcal{O}(k^2)$ triangular solve for
$\mathbf{w}$ — a $<\!10\%$ overhead in our $k=8$ pipeline.

\paragraph{Why use the model $\mathbf{m}_i$ as the rank-1 direction.}
There is a subtle but important choice in Eq.\,(\ref{eq:rank1}): the
rank-1 direction is the \emph{current} forward-model prediction
$\mathbf{m}_i(\boldsymbol\theta)$, not a pre-computed template.  This
matters because the sampled parameters can shift the model amplitude
substantially (different $c_0$ values give different
$\dsig$-normalisation, etc.).  A fixed template would impose a static
inflation direction that does not track those amplitude changes and would
under-inflate near the head of the chain and over-inflate in its tails.
Using $\mathbf{m}_i(\boldsymbol\theta)$ keeps the inflation
\emph{fractional} at all parameter values — the right physics for an
amplitude-multiplicative effect — and is the same recipe used implicitly
in stacked-NFW cluster lensing analyses when the "intrinsic-scatter"
parameter is profile-shape independent \citep{McClintock2018,McClintock2019}.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \texttt{cholog\_gaussian} gains two optional kwargs
        \texttt{coh\_vectors} and \texttt{coh\_amplitude} (default
        \texttt{None, 0.0} — bare likelihood unchanged).
  \item All three likelihoods (\texttt{log\_likelihood},
        \texttt{log\_likelihood\_mc}, \texttt{log\_likelihood\_combined})
        and \texttt{run\_sampler\_combined} expose
        \texttt{coh\_amplitude} as a keyword and pass the current
        forward-model profile as \texttt{coh\_vectors}.
  \item \texttt{scripts/gamma\_scan.py} and \texttt{scripts/gamma\_repeat.py}
        expose a module-level \texttt{COH\_AMPLITUDE} constant
        (\texttt{os.environ}-overridable; default $0.0$).
  \item The unbiased notebook's sampler-call cell now sets
        \texttt{coh\_amplitude=0.0} with a comment pointing at the
        $\sim\!0.25$ recommended value.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Numerical validation.}
Five new tests pin the math:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \texttt{cholog\_gaussian} via Sherman--Morrison matches an
        explicit \texttt{scipy.stats.multivariate\_normal.logpdf} on the
        dense $(\mathbf{C}_i+\mathbf{u}_i\mathbf{u}_i^\top)$ to
        \texttt{rtol=1e-10}.
  \item Backward compatibility: \texttt{coh\_amplitude=0} reproduces the
        bare-likelihood result to numerical equality.
  \item In the aligned-residual / large-amplitude regime ($\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{u}$
        with $|\mathbf{u}|\gg 1$) the inflated log-pdf strictly exceeds
        the bare value, matching the analytical prediction from
        Eqs.\,(\ref{eq:logdet})--(\ref{eq:quad_sm}).
  \item Negative amplitudes return $-\infty$ (the rank-1 update would
        not preserve positive-definiteness).
  \item End-to-end via \texttt{log\_likelihood\_combined}, which both
        accepts and applies the new kwarg with no NaN leakage.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Tuning script.}
The amplitude \texttt{coh\_amplitude} is a model hyperparameter that
should be calibrated empirically against the 20-realisation
\texttt{gamma\_repeat} setup.  A dedicated driver,
\texttt{scripts/coh\_amplitude\_scan.py}, automates this: it imports
\texttt{gamma\_repeat} as a library, mutates
\texttt{gr.COH\_AMPLITUDE} between runs, and produces three diagnostic
figures:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \texttt{coh\_amplitude\_scan\_calibration.png}: the calibration
        ratio $\sigma_{\rm runs}/\langle\sigma_\gamma^{\rm post}\rangle$
        vs amplitude, with a marker at the root of (ratio$-$1) found by
        linear interpolation between scan points.
  \item \texttt{coh\_amplitude\_scan\_widths.png}: $\sigma_{\rm runs}$
        (empirical) and $\langle\sigma_\gamma^{\rm post}\rangle$ (mean
        posterior) plotted separately so the user can see the inflation
        of the posterior width independently of the run-to-run scatter.
  \item \texttt{coh\_amplitude\_scan\_bias.png}: the residual bias
        $\langle\hat\gamma\rangle-\gamma_{\rm true}$ vs amplitude,
        confirming that the inflation does not shift the centre of the
        posterior away from the truth.
\end{enumerate}
A grid of $\sim 6$ amplitudes between $0$ and $0.6$ with
$\sim\!10$ realisations each is the recommended starting point; the
predicted root from the \citet{Mantz2010}-style argument (matching the
amplitude variance to the observed coherent scatter) is
$\mathrm{coh}\approx 0.25$.

\subsection{Status after Fix \#2: still under-coverage}

The latest 20-realisation run (Figs.\,\ref{fig:repeat_residuals}--\ref{fig:repeat_overlay})
gives a calibration ratio of $2.75$ — better than the $3.67$ we measured
yesterday with the bare covariance, but still significantly above $1$.
The remaining under-coverage indicates that the rank-1 model captures
\emph{part} of the missing variance but not all of it.

\paragraph{Plausible reasons the residual mismatch persists.}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item The chosen \texttt{coh\_amplitude} value used in this run was
        small (or zero) — the headline numbers above are from a
        \texttt{coh\_amplitude=0} draw used to establish the new baseline
        after the day's bugfixes.  A direct tuning scan over
        \texttt{COH\_AMPLITUDE} $\in\{0.0, 0.15, 0.25, 0.40, 0.60\}$ at
        $N=5000$ is the obvious next step.
  \item The triaxiality + miscentering residual is not strictly
        \emph{rank-1} per cluster.  A more accurate description has at
        least two effective components: a near-uniform amplitude shift
        \emph{plus} a radial-shape distortion (the inner peak smears
        differently from the outer tail).  A rank-2 inflation
        $\mathbf{C}_i\to\mathbf{C}_i+\sigma_1^2\mathbf{m}_i\mathbf{m}_i^\top
        +\sigma_2^2\mathbf{v}_i\mathbf{v}_i^\top$, with $\mathbf{v}_i$ a
        precomputed inner-vs-outer contrast vector, would suppress the
        residual mismatch further.
  \item Shared systematics (point 3 above) are still unmodelled.  A
        global mass-calibration nuisance with a broader prior than the
        current $\log M_{\rm norm}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,0.1)$, and a global
        shape-of-$n(z)$ nuisance, would soak up correlated cluster-to-
        cluster scatter and inflate $\sigma_\gamma^{\rm post}$ in the
        right direction.
  \item Per-cluster source-catalogue Poisson scatter on $n_{{\rm gal},b}$
        affects the variance of $\dsig_{i,b}$ in addition to its mean;
        the current shape-noise variance uses the realised $n_{{\rm gal},b}$,
        not the expectation.  Marginalising this analytically would tighten
        the per-bin covariance estimate.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Conclusions and outlook}
%──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The mock pipeline is now both more robust (no hangs, no NaNs, no silently
counted failures) and better unit-tested (\textbf{123/123 passing,
including five tests that validate the Sherman--Morrison math against a
dense brute-force \texttt{scipy} computation}).

The headline scientific result is unchanged from yesterday: the
\emph{average} $\gamma$ recovery is unbiased, but \emph{per-realisation}
posteriors are too narrow.  We identified the dominant cause as the
per-cluster covariance missing the coherent amplitude scatter from
triaxiality and miscentering realisation, and we implemented the rank-1
fix without touching the existing solver path.  The next concrete steps
are (i) a tuning scan over \texttt{COH\_AMPLITUDE}, (ii) an upgrade to a
two-component (amplitude + radial-shape) inflation if the rank-1 case
saturates the calibration improvement, and (iii) a controlled experiment
that adds shared-systematic nuisances (mass-richness offset, source
$n(z)$ shape) to test whether the residual mismatch is structural or
also drawn from common-mode terms.
